# Call

What exactly does a HopIn call promise, from the pre-join to the end screens, and which test proves each rule?

## Pre-join

Ruled by the operator:

- CALL-26: Before I turn my camera on, nobody receives any picture from me.
- CALL-27: "Kamera ausschalten" releases the camera, so its light goes off; turning it on opens the camera again.
- CALL-28: The pre-join preview starts with camera and microphone on and stays on the device; nothing reaches the server before Join.
- CALL-29: The call starts with what is on at the pre-join, and what is switched off there stays off in the call; the Start or Join button names that state, such as "Mit Kamera und Mikrofon beitreten".
- CALL-30: A guest who opens a call link gets the same pre-join as the host.
- CALL-31: On a desktop, the HopIn brand leads back from the pre-join to the start page.
- CALL-32: The pre-join's own row shows the account's name, and "Du" until the name is known.
- CALL-33: The address becomes the call link when the call starts.

## Call and end screens

Ruled by the operator:

- CALL-01: The host is the signed-in account, not a connection. When the host's last connection drops, the call waits 60 seconds for the host and then ends for everyone.
- CALL-02: A call has no password. The host can lock a running call so that nobody new can join, and unlock it again.
- CALL-03: Calls use a STUN server and, when a direct connection fails, a TURN relay. The provider is configuration; no page code names it.
- CALL-04: Only signed-in members get TURN credentials. Each call start asks the provider for a fresh credential that lives 12 hours and is never refreshed.
- CALL-05: The operator switches TURN on or off in HopIn's configuration; off means STUN only. With TURN on and its key missing or unusable, HopIn refuses to start.
- CALL-06: While the host is away (the 60 seconds after the host's connection dropped), members can still join an unlocked call.
- CALL-07: An away host keeps a place, so a newcomer cannot take the last of the six places while the host is away.
- CALL-08: A broken connection between two people reconnects by itself, without anyone leaving or rejoining, and HopIn keeps trying for as long as both are in the call.
- CALL-09: In bigger calls each picture gets softer, so a phone's total video upload stays at about 2 Mbit/s and the sound stays clear.
- CALL-10: When someone's connection to HopIn drops briefly, they keep their place for 30 seconds while the others see their picture frozen; back within that time, nobody sees them leave and join again.
- CALL-11: For a host whose connection drops, the 60 seconds count from the drop.
- CALL-12: People in a call see each other's Keycloak username on the tiles and in chat (not on a strip tile, CALL-55).
- CALL-13: The same account in one call twice (phone and laptop) joins as two people, and stays host while any of its connections is in the call.
- CALL-14: A call link whose call has ended, or an address that never had a call, shows "Dieser Link ist abgelaufen" when someone joins; HopIn keeps no store of ended calls, so both are the same case.
- CALL-15: The start page is `/` and a call is `/c/{id}`; the old addresses `/call` and `/join/{id}` answer not found.
- CALL-16: "Zurück zum Start" opens the start page as a new page; the browser's back button then returns to the call link, which shows the call as it is now.
- CALL-17: When the host ends the call, everyone else sees "Der Anruf ist beendet" and that the host, by name, ended the call for everyone.
- CALL-18: A host who leaves sees "Du hast den Anruf verlassen" with only "Zurück zum Start"; the call goes on for the others for up to 60 seconds.
- CALL-19: "Wieder beitreten" and "Erneut versuchen" open the call link again, as tapping it in a message does; if the call is over, the person sees "Dieser Link ist abgelaufen".
- CALL-20: When a call ends for me, or HopIn refuses to let me in, my camera and microphone turn off at once.
- CALL-21: A person with the camera off shows as a neutral person icon, the same for everyone.
- CALL-22: On a phone, the small own picture can be dragged to any corner; it snaps to the nearest one and stays there for the rest of the call, also when people join or leave.
- CALL-23: Everyone in a call sees, on that person's tile, who has muted the microphone or turned the camera off; people who join later see it too, and I see my own mute mark on my own tile.
- CALL-24: After I stop sharing my screen, my camera comes back only if it was on before.
- CALL-25: Without a camera I still see and hear everyone; when I turn my camera on later, everyone in the call sees it, those who were there before me and those who joined after me.
- CALL-34: Alone in the call, the host sees "Warten auf andere" with a way to invite: "Link kopieren", and on a phone "Teilen", which opens the system share menu. No share box stands in the call otherwise.
- CALL-35: HopIn writes the clipboard only when the person presses Copy.
- CALL-36: The call's controls are round buttons floating over the video. On a phone: mic, camera, invite, chat, more, leave in one row at the bottom. On a desktop: mic, camera, screen | invite, chat, more | leave, centred, with "Verlassen" as a pill. Screen sharing exists only on a desktop.
- CALL-37: The number on the Chat button disappears when the chat opens.
- CALL-38: The tile of another person who speaks carries a pulsing accent ring and a three-bar wave in the name pill; the mute mark takes the wave's place while muted; on a phone with two people the full tile shows only the wave; a strip tile shows no name pill (CALL-55).
- CALL-39: In a running call, Impressum and Datenschutz are in "Mehr", one tap away; the call screen shows no account button.
- CALL-40: Only the host sees "Anruf sperren"; a locked call shows "Gesperrt" at the top for everyone.
- CALL-41: A toast fades in and out again over 2.4 seconds.
- CALL-42: One account is in at most 8 calls at once, tabs and devices together. The 9th is refused without retrying and shows "Zu viele offene Anrufe" with "Dein Konto ist schon in 8 Anrufen. Verlass einen, um diesem beizutreten."; a reconnect that meets the limit ends the same way.
- CALL-44: A long chat scrolls inside its panel, and the newest line and the composer stay reachable.
- CALL-45: A tab opened before a release still starts a call: it reloads itself when the new build lacks a piece it needs.
- CALL-46: The end screens name the host by the account's name, and say "Der Host" only when that name is empty.
- CALL-47: An agent takes a place of the call like a person: a call's places (`HOPIN_MAX_PARTICIPANTS_PER_ROOM`) are shared by people and joined agents, and the call has no limit of its own for agents. A person's join and an agent's join are refused as full only when every place is taken, the place CALL-07 keeps for an away host counted as taken, and a place an agent leaves is open to the next person or agent; an invited agent holds no place until it accepts. The stage shows every person and every agent in the call (the quiz master and the agents that joined, a person's own or one with an account of its own), so at most one tile more than the call has places, and the participant count names people only; the call controls stay uncovered.
- CALL-48: A person's own agent joins only a call in which that person holds exactly one live seat, is bound to that seat, and leaves when that seat leaves for good; while the seat waits to resume (CALL-10) the tile stays and the agent's calls answer that the sponsor is away.
- CALL-49: The agent's sponsor or the call's host removes an agent from its tile; a removal by anyone else is refused, and other guests see no remove button on the tile.
- CALL-50: A dialed-in agent shows under the name its sponsor gave it (1 to 32 characters after trimming, no control character), with a "KI" badge and its sponsor's name, so an agent named like a person is still told apart; a name that is no name is refused and no tile shows; a strip tile shows none of them (CALL-55).
- CALL-51: An owner's agent is called through the shared agent control of the fleet's calling standard (the agents repository's `docs/calling.md`), in "Mehr". A member of the realm group that names who may add an owner's agent, and the call's host, are shown the control, and anyone for whom it lists an agent; with no agent to list it shows its one sentence. The display rests on one read of the person's groups per seat and never waits on a fresh one: the add reads fresh, and a person a stale display shows the control is refused as not allowed. While the owner holds exactly one seat in the call, the control lists the agent as available on each such member's seat and on no other, and an add request from any other seat is refused. A seat sponsors every agent of its owner the call has places for (CALL-47): while one of the owner's agents is joined, the owner's other agent is still listed and can be added, and only a call with no place free refuses an add as place full. When no place is free the offers frame carries `full`, and the control shows the kit's place-full sentence in place of its empty sentence. The control lists an agent only while the agent listens on its standing: a lease alone lists none, and the listing ends once the agent's last listen has ended and the agents library's allowance has passed. An add or an accept checks the agent's lease, not its listen. The caller's row shows the agent as inviting and offers no call while the invitation is open; an ended row of an agent the control no longer lists (its owner is gone) stays for its adder; no other member is offered the agent meanwhile. A refused add shows as one of the control's reasons, in the control's words: not allowed, owner absent, place full, agent unavailable or try again; it stays until the person calls again, closes "Mehr" or the next offers frame arrives. A refused add that changes the offers sends the offers frame before the refusal, so a refusal that comes with the frame taking the control away stays readable, and every offers frame after a refusal is a later one.
- CALL-52: The invitation belongs to the owner's one seat and lasts the calling standard's `INVITATION_SECONDS` (the agents library's `agents.member`); the agent accepts it with its own call and then shows as a tile under its own name with the AI mark and the owner as sponsor, taking a place of the call (CALL-47); an accept into a call with no place free is refused as busy. The accept may name the agent's voices in order of preference; the ones speech's catalogue names are kept with the joined agent in that order (CALL-60), and any other voice does not refuse the join. It ends with the sponsor's or the host's removal, when the owner's seat leaves for good, when the agent stops calling for 120 seconds, or when the call ends; a removal, a withdrawal or an expiry makes an older invitation stale, so an accept after it changes nothing. An agent that is at its own limit of places declines the invitation with `decline_invitation(place, invitation, epoch, reason)` and the reason `busy` (not an accept's `busy`, which is a full place); a decline of an invitation that is not the agent's or no longer stands is refused as `stale_invitation` and changes nothing, and a standing decline ends the invitation at once, as its expiry does. An invitation ends without the agent joining by running out (`no_answer`), by a decline (`busy`) or because the owner's seat left before the agent joined (`owner_absent`); it tells the seat that made it, and no other, with `agent_invitation_ended` and that outcome, with the agent's name, after the offers frame that follows the end; the agent control shows the outcome on that agent's row as the kit's no answer, busy or owner away (the kit's duration). A no answer or busy row stays until the seat calls the agent again; an owner away row shows at once, also while the agent is not listed, and goes when the agent is listed again (its owner is back). Every ended row goes when the control is taken away or More is closed. An accept or a decline whose arguments the agents contract refuses (a key it forbids, a malformed epoch or place, a figure its record refuses) is answered by the agents library before HopIn's handler runs, as a tool error that names the failing field paths and never a value, with no refusal code, so the invitation stands and the agent can send a correct call. Tests: `tests/participation/test_feature_own_account.py` (`TestAMalformedInvitationCall`: the tool error at `/mcp` with the field path and no value, and a correct call after it, per tool).
- CALL-53: An agent that holds a joined place in the call, a person's own or one with an account of its own, writes a line with `say`, and every seat reads it in the chat under the name its tile shows, with the AI mark, as plain text. A line holds 1 to 600 characters after trimming and no control character, a line break included; an agent's lines are never refused for how many it wrote in a time window. An empty or longer line (`bad_line`) and a line from an agent with no joined place there (`gone`, an invited agent included) are refused to the agent alone, and no seat sees anything. The call keeps no chat history for seats, so a seat that joins later reads none of the earlier lines; the agents joined in the call read it in their windows (CALL-54).
- CALL-54: An agent that holds a joined place in the call, of either kind, reads every line a person says there after its join, as a person does and without being asked, through the resource of the call's window: it lists the resource, is woken through `subscriptions/listen` when a line is added and reads the window. The window holds only the newest lines, within a bound of count and size; each line has a `seq` counted from 1 within the window's `generation`, the person's or agent's name as the call showed it when the line was said, its kind (`person` or `agent`) and, for the agent's own line, `own`. A person's line is added for the agents the sender's page shows as joined when it is said, which sends it to HopIn's participation only while it shows one (CALL-71), so a line said before the page shows the join is not read; an empty or non-UTF-8 line or name, or a line beyond CALL-71's limits, takes no `seq`; evicted lines show as a `starts_at` beyond the last `seq` read plus one. The window is kept in memory only, goes with the agent's place, is read only through the agent's own principal (a person's own agent through its person, an agent with an account of its own through that account), and holds no line of the game. A person's own agent and an agent with an account of its own are read the same way. While people talk over a sentence of the agent the window also holds `talked_over`: when a person the agent hears (CALL-57) starts speaking while a sentence of the agent plays or waits (CALL-60), or while it is held (CALL-69), it holds an `id`, `playing` and `speech`, the people in the order they started, each with the name the call shows and the words heard so far (empty at first). The `id` is the window's own, never repeats within its `generation` and stays the same from the first person's start until the last person's turn has ended; a second person who starts meanwhile joins it. `playing` says whether a sentence of the agent plays: false when the record opens, because the agent is held at once (CALL-69), true again when a sentence starts or the held one goes on (CALL-70) while the record stands, and false again in the same window change in which that sentence enters the window, unless the agent's next sentence starts at once. A person's finished line replaces their words in the same change, so one read never shows both. Their words go with their turn, an empty one included, with the switch that stops the agent hearing them and with the end of their hearing hop; a turn that began before the agent had a sentence that plays or waits, and is not held, makes no record. A spoken line of the agent enters its window and the others when its sentence has ended or been cut (CALL-60), `cut` marking a cut one, which holds the whole sentence because HopIn cannot tell how far it played. Tests for `talked_over`: `tests/participation/test_chat_window.py` (the record's state, `playing` as its reader gives it), `tests/participation/test_feature_talked_over.py` (who is talked over, the words, the turn's end, a turn that began before the sentence, `playing`) and `tests/participation/test_hearing_talked_over.py` (the same through the hearing route and `/mcp`).
- CALL-55: While a view fills the stage, a strip tile at every width shows no name pill, so neither a name, the wave, the mute mark, the "KI" badge nor the sponsor; an agent's tile keeps its sparkle.
- CALL-56: An agent with a figure from an allowed source shows it idle in its tile's video layer, drawn on the page of each seat, never as a stream; while the figure loads the tile shows its symbol with a ring, any failure (`unsupported`, `refused`, `lost`, `slow`, or a figure that cannot be fetched or loaded, which is `unloaded`) brings back the symbol for good and leaves its reason on the tile as `data-figure-end`, and the agent's removal or the call's end takes the figure away. The seat's browser reports that reason as the closed code in an `agent_figure_ended` frame with the agent's id; HopIn answers nothing and writes the log line `agent_figure.ended` with the room reference, the agent's id and the code, never anything else, once per seat and agent while the agent stays joined. A code outside the five is an invalid message; a report for an agent not joined in the seat's room, or a repeat, is dropped. A call with no allowed figure source requests no figure code. Tests for the end reason: `tests/participation/test_feature_figure_end.py` (each code logged once with ids only, no answer, a bad code, a foreign, unjoined or departed agent, a repeat, one line per seat), `frontend/src/lib/features/figure/index.test.ts` (each ending's reason), `frontend/src/lib/ui/stage.svelte.test.ts` (the tile's attribute) and `frontend/src/lib/features/participation/index.test.ts` (the frame sent).
- CALL-57: Whether a joined agent may hear a person is that person's decision, never the agent's: only the sponsor of a joined agent has a switch for it, which starts on when the sponsor brought the agent (dialed it in, or added it from their own seat) and off when another member added it, and the sponsor's `agent_hearing` frame sets it and is answered with the server's record. The server's record of who may hear whom is the agents library's (its `docs/hearing.md`; the app's part, HEARING-1, -6 to -9, -11, -12 and -14, is proved here): every switch change and the join default are recorded only for a joined agent, a sentence is heard only by an agent whose switch was on no later than the sentence began, and a heard sentence joins the window of each agent that hears it as a spoken line of kind person under the name the call shows for the seat, within the window's bounds (CALL-54). A seat gets one sound session at a time, only while its account holds it in a room, it is not parked and a joined agent hears it; a newer session ends the older, and the session ends with the last agent that hears the seat, and with the agent that serves it (the one whose token it uses, chosen among the agents that hear the seat when it opens) even while another agent still hears the seat, with the seat's park, and with the seat's leaving. Pausing an agent does not end hearing and a parked seat is not an end; the agent's removal or leave and its lease's end, the seat's leaving for good, and the call's end and shutdown each end hearing at once, so nothing that finishes afterwards is appended, and a rejoined agent hears only what began after its new join. While the setting that lets agents hear is off, the frame is refused as unknown, no switch is ever on and nobody is heard. The browser half: only the sponsor's browser gets the `agent_hearing` record, and it shows the record as design's switch „Agent darf mich hören“ at the top left of the agent's tile, clear of the remove button, on a strip tile too and never hidden while the tile shows (under 131 px of tile width, as a strip tile can be, the switch stands at the tile's lower right, 2 px from its right and bottom edges, its track whole and pressable and its icon cut; under a coarse pointer the chip's touch margin is given back, so its drawn chip keeps its place, the remove button stays above its target and its target is clipped by the tile); a press only asks the server, and the switch shows what the server answers. A browser that gets no record, a non-sponsor's or any with the setting off, shows no switch. The ear mark on the tiles of the people an agent hears is not drawn yet.
- CALL-58: A sponsor's microphone sound reaches an agent only through HopIn's server, over the hearing socket of the sponsor's own seat (`/ws/hearing/<seat>`), and only while the setting that lets agents hear is on; with it off the route does not exist and nobody is called. The socket is admitted as `/ws` is, by a session and an allowed Origin, and one without is refused before it is accepted. It gets a hop only for a seat its account holds in a room, which is not parked and which a joined agent hears (CALL-57); otherwise it is closed with the code of its refusal (`no_seat`, `seat_away`, `nobody_hears`). It gets one too only while HopIn holds the token exchanged for that agent, else it is closed with `no_token` and neither Keycloak nor the speech service is called. HopIn forwards the browser's sound frames unread to the speech service, with the agent's exchanged token and its own workload token and with no loudness gate; both tokens are read afresh at every opening of the service's session, so a session the service drained or cut is reopened by the speech client with the tokens HopIn holds then, each sentence enters a window once and none is dropped by the reopening, the hop ends in order when the hop has ended, the agent's token is gone or the issuer refuses the workload token (the refusal is logged by its closed code), and with its service code when the client's reconnect bound runs out: the speech service alone decides what is speech, in German or English, and each finished sentence enters the window of each agent that hears the seat (CALL-54), of the two a seat may sponsor, and of no other as a spoken line of kind person under the speaker's shown name; a delta or an empty transcript enters no line, but the moment a person starts speaking, the words heard so far and the end of each turn, an empty one included, reach the windows of those agents as the record of people talking over them (CALL-54). The browser is sent no token and no text, only the code its socket closes with (`HearingClose`): a normal close when the hop ends in order, a switch off or any other end of CALL-57 included, and its own code when the speech service is busy or fails, when the sound held for the service passes the sound of the speech client's opening bound (`HELD_FRAMES` hop frames, derived from the client's `NEEDED_HOLD_BYTES` and its hop frame, not restated), when a frame is text or over one hop frame, and when the hop passes its deadline of one hour. Every end of CALL-57 ends the hop at once, the speech session with it, also while HopIn still awaits its own workload token, so a hop lives only while its agent's lease does and a sentence that finishes afterwards is not appended. The socket's own session is checked again every `SESSION_RECHECK_SECONDS`, and a hop whose session has ended or cannot be checked ends like any other end. One limit stays (speech#311): frames the relay accepted before the end and still holds, at most `HELD_LIMIT_BYTES` (`HELD_FRAMES` hop frames), may still be written to the speech service after the end; it is sound spoken while the switch was on, and no text of it reaches the agent after the end, so a sentence that ends after a switch-off enters no window. The hold is the client's `NEEDED_HOLD_BYTES`, which covers its opening bound and a whole reconnect, so a speech session that opens late, within it, or a stall of the service shorter than it, keeps the person heard and does not end the hop with `over_cap`. HopIn logs one closed outcome code per hop, and never sound, text or a token. HopIn's own workload token is fetched at the issuer once for the hops that follow within its lifetime and anew once it is near its end, and a refused fetch is not kept, so the next hop asks again. Tests: `tests/participation/test_hearing_route.py` (the route at the real `/ws` composition with a fake speech service, the hold against a session that opens late and the end past it), `test_hearing_reopening.py` (the reopened session with its tokens and its two endings), `test_feature_hearing.py` and `test_hearing_talked_over.py` (the windows and the record of people talking over an agent).
- CALL-59: The browser half of CALL-58. A sponsor's browser sends its microphone sound to the hearing socket of its own seat only while the server's record of its switch (CALL-57) is on for at least one of its agents and the call core holds its microphone, and sends nothing in the pre-join, for a seat that gets no record, after the hearing agent's leave, after the seat's new join and after the call's end. The core tells a feature's microphone handler (`FeatureCore.onMicrophone`) its state when the handler registers and on each change: a stream that holds only the local microphone track while the person is seated in the call and their microphone is on, and `null` in every other state (the pre-join, the microphone off, the local stream replaced or stopped, the call's end); never a screen share's audio, a peer's or an agent's. A new local stream starts with the microphone on, so a second call in the same page reports the track's real state. The capture is speech's browser package, one capture per hop, with no loudness gate, its worklet served by the page build as a file of its own under `/_app` and the hop at `/ws/hearing/<seat>` of the page's origin, both inside the page's Content-Security-Policy as it stands. It stops when the person presses a switch off (without waiting for the server's answer, which a refusal may leave on; once the server answers, it runs again if an agent still hears the person, the other agent of two included, and a later record of on starts it again; the stop still sends the last partial frame, of sound spoken before the press, at most 0.1 s, and nothing spoken after the press is sent), when the microphone goes away or its stream is replaced, when the hearing agent leaves, when a new seat is announced and when the call ends; with two agents it runs until the last that hears the person is off. A hop that ends past its deadline, over the server's hold of sound (`over_cap`, CALL-58), or in order while an agent still hears the person (the end of the hop's serving agent, CALL-58), is opened once more, and so is a capture whose audio context leaves running after it streamed (a failed sound device; speech's local end `context-stopped`), each change of how many agents hear the person allowing one more reopening; any other end, a refusal included, leaves the capture closed until the next change of how many agents hear the person, or of the microphone, which starts a new one (not a record that leaves that number as it was), and nothing tells the person that nobody hears them. A microphone track that ends by itself ends the package's capture; the core does not handle it. Tests: `frontend/src/lib/features/participation/hearing.test.ts` (the reopening of each end, its budget of one per change, the ends that stay closed) and `frontend/src/lib/call/media.test.ts` and `index.test.ts` (the microphone state the capture reads) and `tests/e2e/participation/test_agent_hears_sponsor.py` (the sound reaches the agent in a real browser, and a stopped audio context reopens the hop once).
- CALL-60: An agent with an account of its own that holds an active joined place in the call (its sponsor's seat is connected) says one sentence aloud with `speak(place, text)`, which serves both kinds of agent but succeeds only for one HopIn holds an exchanged token for (CALL-58), and only while the setting that lets agents speak is on. The sentence is rendered by the speech service in the first voice of the list the agent's accept named (the ones speech's catalogue names, in the accept's order) that the service's health offers ready in the sentence's language, read for each sentence, else, when the accept named no voice speech's catalogue names, in the configured voice (`HOPIN_SPEECH__VOICE`, which must speak German), in the language the call names as `language` (a two-letter code such as `en`; HEARING-20 of the agents' contract) or, when it names none, in the page's language, German, under the agent's own account and HopIn's workload token, and `speak` answers `Spoken(spoken)` at the moment its sentence starts (HEARING-15 of the agents' contract), not when it is queued and not when its sound is whole: the speech client streams the WAV (`speak_stream_with_preference`), HopIn keeps each chunk in the sentence's sound as it arrives, and the sentence starts at its first chunk, once its turn has come. The sentence's length is the exact one the stream tells at its end (counted from the sound's PCM bytes, so a streaming WAV that declares no size is as long as the frames it holds), and the sentence ends when that length has run out on the server's clock since it started, so a call that waits behind the agent's earlier sentences answers when the previous one has ended and a refusal answers at once. Each agent has one queue, in memory only, in the order their first chunks arrived, and an agent has at most 10 sentences waiting behind the one that plays, those still being rendered counted among the waiting: a sentence starts when the previous one has ended, never earlier, also when the speech service still renders that one. When it starts it stands in the call's chat once as the agent's line, as `say` writes one, and every seat gets `agent_voice` with the agent's id and the voice's id; a browser that joins later hears only the sentences that start after it joined. When it has ended, or been cut by a stop or by an end of the agent's place, it enters the window of its own agent as `own` and `spoken` and the window of every other joined agent as a `spoken` line under the agent's shown name (CALL-54), in every window with `cut` when cut and with `continues` when it ended with the same agent's next sentence already waiting to play, which starts at once (never on a cut line, a sentence that ended last in its queue or a person's line). The sound is fetched at `GET /api/game/voice/<id>` by a signed-in account that holds a seat in the sound's room, answered `no-store` and streamed: every reader gets the sound from its first chunk, then each chunk as it arrives, and the response ends where the sound ends; a sound whose stream failed ends the response where it broke without a clean end, so a player reports it broken, and one that a stop or an end dropped ends it where it stands. Any other account, a sound already played and an id nobody was told get 404. The sound grows in memory only, within the size cap of the speech client's call (`cap_bytes`), and stays until the sentence has ended or been cut.
